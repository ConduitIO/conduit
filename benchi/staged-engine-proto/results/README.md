# Local results: v1 against arch-v2 main against the prototype

**LOCAL, INDICATIVE, NOT GATE EVIDENCE.** Docker Desktop VM (16 CPUs) on a shared Apple M3 Max laptop whose own load
average was 5 to 15 throughout (other work, other agent sessions). The graduation gate needs quiesced hardware.
Windows are 15 to 25 s, not the harness's 60 s, so floors are wider than the gate's. Rates from different sessions are
not comparable; compare arms inside a session only.

Arms: `v1` (main, classic engine), `v2-main` (main with `--preview.pipeline-arch-v2`), `v2-proto` (this branch, same
flag). Main is `c60003bb`; the prototype is its child. Method and caveats: `../README.md`. Each session's `runs.csv`,
`summary.md` and `env.json` are in `sessions/`. "A/A max" is the largest per-round difference between the two runs of
the same arm in that session; a difference between arms smaller than the relevant A/A max is not resolved.

Records are counted at the sink, per sink (so at 2x2 and 4x4 the engine writes 2x and 4x as many records as the
number shown).

## How to read it

**The three gate shapes (01b, 02, 03), default config, builtin connectors.**

- 1x1: the prototype is above v1 by +8.7% (the quiet control session 16: +10.4% with floors of 1.2% or less) and
  above arch-v2 main by +36% to +38%. arch-v2 main is 19% to 21% below v1, which reproduces the AWS read (-25%) in
  direction and rough size. Session 01b has two disturbed rounds (host load 14); the A/A max is correspondingly
  wide, the medians are robust.
- 2x2: the prototype is +42% over v1 and +40% over arch-v2 main; arch-v2 main and v1 are level (+1.9%, inside the
  7.5% floor), so the AWS read's -6% is not resolved here.
- 4x4: v1 is the slowest by far (28.4k per sink): arch-v2 main is +51%, the prototype +73%.

**These shapes are bounded by the connector SDK, and at 1x1 by how the in-memory stream hands records over.**

- Sessions 15 and 16 (same binaries, one environment variable): with the builtin in-memory stream unbuffered (the
  production value) the 1x1 numbers are 65.9k / 53.6k / 72.7k. With a 64-slot buffer on the stream's channels **all
  three engines run at 97k to 100k, within 2.5%**. The 1x1 differences between engines come from goroutine hand-offs
  between the engine and the plugin, not from the engines' own work. This is a diagnostic patch
  (`patches/stream-buffer.patch`), not part of the prototype.
- Session 09: with SDK schema extraction off on both connectors, arch-v2 main and the prototype are level at 122k and
  both are +48% over v1; only at 4x4 (session 10) does the prototype pull ahead (+11.5%).
- Session 06 (Alternative A): a source batch size of 100 makes arch-v2 main +31% over v1, and the prototype adds nothing
  over arch-v2 main (+0.1%).
- Session 12 (acks arrive through a buffered pump, which is what a gRPC destination looks like): at 1x1 the prototype
  is 10% above arch-v2 main and **18% below v1** (floors 0.7%). At 2x2 (session 13) it is +24% over v1.

**Where the engine is the difference.**

- Destination latency (04, 05): arch-v2 main falls to 592 rec/s at 1 ms and 94 rec/s at 10 ms (one write per round
  trip). The prototype does not move (56.6k, 56.4k), nor does v1. Same with batching (08): main -16.7% against v1, the
  prototype +23.9%.
- Destination batching with a flush delay (07): arch-v2 main 86 rec/s, v1 57.8k, the prototype 67.6k.
- N sources with a stuck-free destination (02, 03, 13, 17): the `sharedMu` removal is the 2x2 result; with the stream
  buffered (17) the prototype is 2.2x v1 and 3.6x arch-v2 main at 2x2.
- A latency inside `Write` (11) caps every engine at about 1/L: no engine can help there.

**What this does and does not say about the decision rule** ("prototype >= v1 at 1x1 against the A/A floor"):
locally, on builtin connectors with the unbuffered in-memory stream, yes (+9% to +10%, quiet-session floors 1.2%).
That result is fragile: it disappears (-2.5%, everything level at the SDK ceiling) if the stream is buffered, and
reverses (-18%) with buffered acks. The robust wins are N>1, destinations with latency, and destination batching;
the 1x1 default-shape throughput is mostly a property of the connectors and the stream. The AWS run is what decides,
and it should include the shapes that separate those effects (the prepared sessions include source batching and
latency; the stream-buffer and schema-off shapes are local diagnostics and should be added if the 1x1 result is
borderline).

## Disturbances and re-runs, stated

- Session 01 (first 1x1 run) was disturbed in rounds 1 and 2 (a build running on the same machine plus other host
  load); it is kept in `sessions/01-1x1` and replaced by `01b-1x1-rerun` for the table. Its quiet rounds agree
  (v1 64.7k, v2-main 53.7k, proto 70.6k).
- Sessions 06 and 08 were first started with a wrong shape directory name and produced nothing
  (`06-failed-wrong-shape-name`, `08-failed-wrong-shape`); they were re-run unchanged otherwise.
- Sessions 04 to 11 used the latency hook without the 100 us sleep floor; sessions 12 onwards use it (the patch in
  this PR). A single-run check showed no difference (57.3k with and without at 1x1, 1 ms).
- The hook's ack pump is itself a performance factor for the prototype (it decouples the plugin's ack send from the
  AckReader): see sessions 12 and 04/05, where "latency" for the prototype is the same as "pump with no latency".
  Compare the prototype's latency sessions with session 12, not with 01b.
- Host load average at the start of most sessions was 5 to 10; per-run host loads are in `runs.csv`.
